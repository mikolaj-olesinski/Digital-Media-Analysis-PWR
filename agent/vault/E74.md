# E74 · Anomalies: posts ×10 above or below the prediction

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: which posts get ×10 more (or fewer) views than their followers and reactions suggest, and can they be flagged in advance?
- Hypothesis: ×10 above are more often promoted (paid reach) and copies; ×10 below are zero-reaction posts. An author's history flags ×10-above posts.
- Refuted if: no trait differs by ≥ ×1.5 between the groups and the rest.

## Setup
- Over-performance r from the out-of-fold final model; ×10 above: r ≥ 1, ×10 below: r ≤ -1. Shares of our type labels (promo / spam / organic), zero reactions, copied text (E63), brand accounts (E66), repeat authors; median followers, likes, views. Flagging: among posts with an earlier post, P(×10 above) when the author's earlier mean over-performance ≥ 0.5 vs otherwise.

## Result
- ×10 above: 1.3% (TikTok), 5.7% (YouTube), 6.0% (X) of posts; ×10 below: 1.2% / 3.4% / 3.9%.
- ×10 above vs rest: promo 59% vs 34% on TikTok (YouTube, X no difference); copied text 9% vs 1% (TikTok), 5% vs 1% (YouTube), 8% vs 3% (X); brand or brand-named accounts 9% vs 4% (TikTok, YouTube); repeat authors 16% vs 6% (TikTok), 23% vs 12% (X); YouTube: zero reactions 48% vs 24%.
- ×10 below: zero reactions 48% vs 3% (TikTok), 82% vs 24% (YouTube); spam 31% vs 13% on TikTok.
- With history: P(×10 above) is 12.5% vs 1.8% (TikTok, 8 posts flagged), 19% vs 1.3% (YouTube, 78), 32% vs 5.4% (X, 19) when the author over-performed before.

## Decision
Supported. Posts far above the prediction are more often promotional (TikTok), copied campaign texts, brand accounts and repeat authors: consistent with paid or cross-posted reach. Posts far below are mostly zero-reaction posts and spam. An author's history raises the chance of a ×10-above post 6-15×, so it works as a "suspicious reach" flag.
