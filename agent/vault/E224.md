# E224 · Off-brand mentions: is a mention really about its brand?

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: for each of the 12,500 Brand24 mentions, how likely is it that the post is **not** about the brand it was
  matched to (E35: Cricket Wireless mentions that are about the sport), and what do those posts do to the view model?
- Hypothesis (falsifiable): a views-free combined detector (lexical + embedding + text-graph + text classifier) separates
  off-brand posts from on-brand ones, and the off-brand share is large enough to matter for brand totals.
- **Refuted if: AUC of the combined score for `not_about_brand` < 0.75** on the 300-post validation sample (unclear
  labels excluded). Secondary falsifier: no brand with >= 20 mentions above 20% estimated off-brand share.

## Setup
- Data: all 12,500 mentions, 5 platforms; nothing in Phase 1 reads `view_count`, `likes` or `b24_estimated_reach`.
  Views are used only in the last step (model error, brand totals), as an evaluation.
- Phase 1 detectors, each scored so that **high = suspicious**:
  1. lexical: normalised brand name or its joined handle form absent from title + content (binary);
  2. embedding: cosine to the brand's leave-self-out e5 centroid, within-brand robust z (high z = unlike its brand);
  3. graph: TF-IDF 10-NN and e5 10-NN text graphs + Louvain (`graph_lab.knn_graph`, `graph_lab.louvain`); per post the
     smoothed neighbour and community same-brand share against the brand's platform base rate (log lift, sign flipped);
  4. classifier: one-vs-rest logistic regression predicting the brand from TF-IDF, out-of-fold probability of the
     assigned brand (low = suspicious), brands with >= 5 posts on the platform.
  Combined = mean of the percentile ranks of the available detectors over all 12,500 posts.
- Phase 1b: 300-post validation sample, stratified by combined-score decile (15 per decile for 1-5, 25 for 6-8, 50 for 9,
  100 for 10), brands with an ambiguous name (hand list, `brand_industry.csv`) weighted x3, all five platforms.
  Two files: `offbrand_sample.csv` (blind: no scores) and `offbrand_scores.csv` (scores only, no text).
- Phase 2: `agent/offbrand_guide.md`, three independent AI labelling passes (shuffled each time, scores unseen) on the
  sample, fourth adjudication pass on disagreements; Fleiss kappa over the three passes; AUC, precision in the top
  5% / 10% of the combined score, off-brand share per platform and per brand, design-weighted population estimate.
  Labels are AI labels and await a team member's check.
- Rerun: `python -W ignore src/offbrand.py` (scores + sample), then `--stage eval` after the labels exist. Kueue
  `amc-offbrand` 4 CPU / 4Gi.

## Result
All numbers from `data/processed/offbrand_numbers.json`; labels are **AI labels, awaiting a team member's check**.

**Phase 1 (12,500 mentions, no views read).** The lexical detector finds the brand name (or its handle / hashtag form)
in 89.4% of the posts - the keyword that Brand24 matched is usually in the text by construction, so absence is rare.
Embedding alignment check passes (own-brand minus random-brand cosine, median +0.031). Detector Spearman agreement is
low on average (mean pairwise 0.31; graph TF-IDF vs e5 0.54, lexical vs everything 0.02-0.06), so the four detectors
measure different things. 12,276 posts have all four. Phase-1 score ranking, brands with >= 20 mentions by share of
posts in the top decile: HBO Max 0.60, AB InBev 0.55, AT&T 0.50, Huntington Bank 0.43, X 0.42, Twilio 0.39 - a mix of
genuine ambiguity and brands whose corporate name is never used (their products are).

**Validation sample and labels.** 300 posts, all 5 platforms (TikTok 53 / YouTube 59 / X 68 / Facebook 65 / Instagram 55),
by decile 15-100, 135 posts from the 83 ambiguous-name brands, max 10 per brand. Three blind passes + adjudication:
Fleiss kappa **0.811** (pairwise Cohen 0.80-0.82), 88.7% of posts unanimous, 34 disputed, the adjudicator sided with the
majority in 61.8%. Final labels: 221 `not_about_brand`, 66 `about_brand`, 13 `unclear`.

**AUC for `not_about_brand` (unclear excluded, n = 287, 221 positives), unweighted | design-weighted:**

| detector | AUC | 95% CI | design-weighted AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| lexical | 0.475 | 0.422-0.525 | 0.533 |
| embedding | 0.616 | 0.543-0.686 | 0.530 |
| graph TF-IDF | 0.670 | 0.592-0.738 | 0.634 |
| graph e5 | 0.693 | 0.622-0.767 | 0.742 |
| graph (both) | 0.702 | 0.628-0.773 | 0.719 |
| classifier | 0.665 | 0.584-0.747 | 0.686 |
| **combined** | **0.677** | **0.589-0.763** | **0.717** |
| combined without lexical (post-hoc) | 0.697 | - | 0.694 |

**The hypothesis is refuted as pre-registered:** the combined score is 0.677 (design-weighted 0.717), below the 0.75 bar,
and the CI (0.589-0.763) does not exclude 0.75. No single detector reaches 0.75 either; the best is the e5 graph at 0.693
(0.742 weighted). The near-chance lexical detector dilutes the rank average, but dropping it only reaches 0.697.

**Precision in the top of the score.** Top 5% (626 posts in the population, 54 labelled): precision 0.784 (0.741 if
unclear counts as on-brand). Top 10% (1,251 posts, 100 labelled): 0.844 (0.810). So the score is usable as a *flagging*
device at the extreme, even though it does not separate the middle.

**Estimated off-brand share.** Design-weighted over (score decile x ambiguous-name flag):
**64.3% of mentions are not about their brand (95% CI 55.8-72.6)**; 61.5% if unclear counts as on-brand; unclear 4.3%.
A conservative lower bound: 6.7% are off-brand *and* the brand string is not in the text at all. The number is a
definition statement ("the brand must be the subject of the post"): a passing mention, a hashtag-only post, a
retailer or payment-processor mention and the use of another brand's product all count as off-brand, while a product of
the matched brand counts as on-brand. Even the least suspicious deciles are 36-54% off-brand (deciles 1-5 mean about 0.47);
the off-brand rate rises to ~0.87 in deciles 6-10.

Per platform (design-weighted): TikTok 0.588, YouTube 0.708, X 0.638, Facebook 0.443, Instagram 0.773 (labelled counts
53 / 59 / 68 / 65 / 55). Per brand with >= 5 labelled posts: PayPal 10/10, Mollie 9/10, Avanza 9/10, Hootsuite 8/9,
Stripe 8/10, Dodge 6/6, Adidas 7/10, eToro 6/10, Motorola 6/9, Monster Energy 3/10, The North Face 2/10 (12 brands).

**Effect on the view model (TikTok / YouTube / X, out-of-fold predictions of `results_preds.csv`).** Top-5% score posts
against the rest: MAE **0.385** (n = 444, x2.42) vs **0.398** (n = 6,291, x2.50); per platform 0.282 vs 0.272 (TikTok),
0.450 vs 0.463 (YouTube), 0.435 vs 0.485 (X). By final label: `not_about_brand` 0.418 (n = 131) vs `about_brand` 0.380
(n = 49), CIs overlap. Off-brand mentions do not damage the view estimate - the model runs on reactions and author, not
on topic.

**Brand view totals if the top-5% posts are dropped (descriptive).** Median brand keeps 99.8% of its views; 15% of the
319 brands with >= 5 posts lose more than 10%, 8.5% lose more than 25%; all-brand views kept 95%. Largest losses:
Cricket Wireless 50.7% of its views, Subaru 80.7%, Dairy Queen 25.3%, Samsung 34.8%, "Dunkin" 98.3% and "Dunkin'" 80.3%
(two brand strings for one brand), YouTube 51.5%, Authentic Brands Group 40.5%.

## Decision
The 0.75 bar is missed: the combined detector is a *flagging* tool for the extreme top (78% precision in the top 5%),
not a per-mention probability that separates the middle. For the preprint's Applications section the usable statements
are (1) the estimated off-brand share, with its definition and the AI-label caveat, (2) the top-5% / top-10% precision,
and (3) that off-brand mentions cost the view model nothing (MAE 0.42 vs 0.38 by label). B15 is closed as an instrument
(the detector, the blind sample, the guide and the AI labels exist); a human check of the 300 labels stays open as B31.
