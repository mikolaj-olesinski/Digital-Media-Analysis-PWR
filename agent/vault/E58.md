# E58 · Abstention: error on the posts we keep, ranked by predicted uncertainty

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: if Brand24 only shows estimates for the most certain posts, how fast does the error fall? Is the quantile width a better ranking than the reaction tier (E34)?
- Hypothesis: keeping the 50% narrowest intervals lowers MAE by ≥ 25%; the quantile width beats the tier width.

## Setup
- Final model errors ranked by: quantile width (q90 − q10, E57), reaction-tier width (90% residual quantile per tier), random, oracle (true error).
- MAE on the kept share, 10%-100%. Method after Sokol et al. 2024 (abstention curves).

## Result
MAE of the final model on the share of posts with the narrowest interval:

| kept | ranking | TikTok | YouTube | X |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100% | — | 0.273 | 0.456 | 0.466 |
| 50% | quantile width | 0.246 (-10%) | 0.334 (**-27%**) | 0.439 (-6%) |
| 50% | reaction-tier width | 0.248 | 0.348 | 0.448 |
| 50% | random | 0.274 | 0.461 | 0.469 |
| 50% | oracle (true error) | 0.093 | 0.168 | 0.178 |

## Decision
Partly supported: abstaining on the uncertain half cuts the error by 27% on YouTube, but only 10% on TikTok and 6% on X; the quantile width is slightly better than the reaction tier. The predicted uncertainty ranks posts much worse than the oracle, so "show only confident estimates" works on YouTube (where zero-reaction posts are both common and uncertain), barely elsewhere.
