# E72 · Variance components: how much of the error is the author, how much the brand

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: what share of the final model's remaining error is shared by posts of the same author, and by posts of the same brand from other authors?
- Hypothesis: author share ≥ 0.3, brand share ≤ 0.1 (E68 vs E69).
- Refuted if: the author share is below the brand share.

## Setup
- Over-performance r (true − predicted, out-of-fold final model), centred per platform. Pairwise intraclass correlation: sum of r_i r_j over pairs of posts by the same author (or the same brand, different authors) divided by the sum of (r_i² + r_j²) / 2 over those pairs. Also for raw log views. Bootstrap by brand (300 draws; copies of a brand never pair with each other).

## Result
| | TikTok | YouTube | X |
|---|---|---|---|
| error shared by an author | **0.74 (0.61-0.86)**, 103 pairs | **0.63 (0.56-0.76)**, 757 pairs | 0.12 (0.08-0.44), 686 pairs |
| error shared by a brand (other authors) | 0.06 (0.02-0.10) | 0.03 (-0.01-0.06) | 0.08 (0.04-0.11) |
| log views shared by an author | 0.77 | 0.80 | 0.46 |
| log views shared by a brand | 0.06 | 0.09 | 0.07 |

- On X a few prolific authors (up to 45 posts) supply most pairs and are less consistent; weighted by author instead (E68 Spearman) X is as consistent as the others (0.70).

## Decision
Supported. About two thirds of the remaining error on TikTok and YouTube is an author level; the brand explains ≤ 0.08. This is why author history helps (E68, E70) and brand context or similar brands do not (E49, E69). For the paper: the error is mostly "who posted", not "about which brand".
