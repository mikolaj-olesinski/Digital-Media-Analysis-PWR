# E69 · Brand communities and cold start from similar brands

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: do brands form communities (by shared authors and similar text), and does the over-performance of similar brands predict a new brand's (cold start, no posts with views)?
- Hypothesis: communities follow industries; similar-brand over-performance correlates with rho ≤ 0.3.
- Refuted if: rho ≥ 0.3 with CI above zero on every platform.

## Setup
- Graph of 348 brands: edge = shared authors (any platform, count) + cosine similarity of mean embedding to each brand's 5 nearest brands; Louvain (seed fixed).
- Cold start: per platform, brands with ≥ 5 posts; Spearman between a brand's mean over-performance (true − predicted, out-of-fold final model) and that of its 5 most similar brands by text; bootstrap over brands.

## Result
- 2,029 edges, 539 with shared authors; 9 communities, modularity 0.50. They are industries: cars and auto parts; telecom; banks; food and retail chains; airlines, travel and postal; tech hardware; ride hailing and delivery; software and consulting; one mixed community.
- Median views per community 188-418; bias of the final model -0.12 (telecom) to +0.00.
- Cold start: rho 0.27 (0.14-0.39) TikTok, 0.02 (-0.12-0.14) YouTube, 0.12 (-0.03-0.26) X. Brand over-performance spread (sd) 0.16 / 0.31 / 0.28.

## Decision
Supported. Brands group cleanly into industries (a good figure for the paper), but similar brands predict a new brand's over-performance only weakly on TikTok and not on YouTube or X, matching E49 (brand context does not help). Unlike authors (E68), brands have no stable level beyond reactions.
