# E68 · Author over-performance persists: recommending authors and using author history

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: if an author's post gets more views than the final model expects (given followers and reactions), do their later posts too? Can this be used to recommend authors and to correct predictions when the author's earlier posts are known?
- Hypothesis: weak persistence (Spearman ≤ 0.3), since author context added ≤ 0.003 next to reactions (E49) and author history ±0.004 at posting time (E43).
- Refuted if: rho ≥ 0.5 with the CI above 0.3, or the correction gains ≥ 0.05.

## Setup
- Over-performance = true − predicted log10 views (out-of-fold final model). Authors with ≥ 2 posts with views on a platform (61 TikTok, 255 YouTube, 83 X), posts in time order.
- Spearman correlation between the first post and the mean of the later ones, bootstrap over authors; top third vs bottom third of authors by first-post over-performance. Robustness: drop later posts repeating an earlier one (same text, URL or view count); later posts about another brand only.
- Use case: correction = a × mean over-performance of the author's earlier posts; a (0-1) chosen on other brand folds; evaluated on posts with an earlier post; paired brand bootstrap.

## Result
- rho 0.68 (0.49-0.82) TikTok, 0.69 (0.60-0.76) YouTube, 0.70 (0.55-0.82) X. Without repeats: 0.70 / 0.67 / 0.66. YouTube, later posts about another brand only: 0.41 (0.19-0.61, 74 authors).
- Authors in the top third by first-post over-performance get ×4.1 / ×7.2 / ×8.7 more views on their later posts than the bottom third, at equal predicted views.
- Views per follower persist even more: rho 0.82 / 0.80 / 0.90.
- Correction with the author's earlier posts (no repeats): MAE 0.373 → 0.257 on TikTok (+0.116, 0.051-0.188, 63 posts), 0.495 → 0.338 on YouTube (+0.157, 0.106-0.216, 379), 0.528 → 0.430 on X (+0.098, 0.027-0.161, 112).

## Decision
Refuted, strongly: the largest gain in the project. Accounts have a stable "views beyond reactions" level (audience outside followers, autoplay, recommendation reach) that the inputs do not see. E43 / E49 missed it because they used likes and followers of other posts or brand-fold training posts, not the author's own past views. Only 3-16% of sample posts have an earlier post by the same author, so the overall MAE changes little in this sample; in Brand24's full stream most active authors have history. For the paper: an author-level view history is the most valuable extra input; authors can be ranked for recommendation by past over-performance.
