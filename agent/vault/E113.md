# E113 · Multi-task across platforms

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: does a shared trunk with one head per platform, trained jointly, beat per-platform models? Sharing could
  borrow strength across platforms with different levels.
- Hypothesis: a small gain, at least on the smaller platforms.
- Refuted if: sharing is no better or worse.

## Setup
- 6 735 posts with views (TikTok 2 491, YouTube 2 441, X 1 803); features log1p(likes), log1p(comments),
  log10(followers), missing flag.
- Multi-task: shared 2-layer trunk (32) + platform embedding, one linear head per platform, L1 loss, 150 epochs.
- Baselines: per-platform MLP of the same capacity; CatBoost with MAE loss (the final model family).
- 5-fold brand-grouped CV, same folds for all.
- Run: `python src/multitask.py`.

## Result
| platform | multi-task | per-platform MLP | CatBoost |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 0.314 | 0.314 | 0.315 |
| YouTube | 0.538 | 0.523 | **0.518** |
| X | 0.504 | 0.488 | **0.485** |

## Decision
Sharing does not help: the multi-task net is equal on TikTok and worse on YouTube (+0.020) and X (+0.019). This is
consistent with E61 (a pooled model with a platform feature was also neutral-to-worse). Platforms differ in level, and
a shared representation does not remove that. No follow-up.
