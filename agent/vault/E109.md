# E109 · Calibrated brand totals with intervals (X)

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: E107 left the X brand totals low by x1.5 and 49% of brands off by >2x. Does a one-number calibration fitted
  out-of-fold fix that, and is an 80% brand interval honest?
- Hypothesis: calibration removes the level bias and an 80% brand-level interval covers about 80% of held-out brands.
- Refuted if: the calibrated totals are no better or the interval coverage is far off.

## Setup
- X, 1 803 posts with views, 189 brands with >= 5 posts.
- Model: ridge on likes + comments, out-of-fold by brand; brand total = sum of predicted views.
- 5-fold brand-grouped CV: on four folds fit a median calibration factor and an 80% half-width from the brand
  residuals; on the fifth apply them.
- Run: `python src/brand_totals_cal.py`.

## Result
| | share off >2x | median error | bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand24 rule | 96% | 1.05 | +1.05 |
| model, uncalibrated | 49% | 0.30 | -0.18 |
| **model, calibrated** | **43%** | **0.26** | **+0.05** |

- 80% brand interval: coverage **0.793** on held-out brands (nominal 0.80).
- 189 brands.

## Decision
A calibrated brand total with an 80% interval is a defensible product: 43% of brands still off by >2x is far from
perfect, but much better than the rule (96%). Report the interval, not the point. The remaining 43% is the tail: a
brand total is dominated by one or two posts (E96).

## Follow-ups
- Widen the interval for brands whose total is dominated by one post (E96).
- Same for FB (19 brands only) and for a monthly window.
