# E51 · Offset target: learn views per like instead of views

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: trees cannot extrapolate, which fits the over-estimate of the lowest fifth and under-estimate of the top fifth of views (E18) and the TabPFN gain in posts with > 1,000 reactions (E47). Does learning log10(views) - log10(likes + 1) and adding the offset back fix part of this?
- Hypothesis: the offset target lowers the bias of the top and bottom fifths by ≥ 0.05 and overall MAE by ≥ 0.005, most on TikTok.
- Refuted if: overall MAE is worse with a CI below zero.

## Setup
- Same features, folds and CatBoost settings as the final model; only the target changes. Also offset + all E49 context.
- Signed error by fifth of true views; paired brand bootstrap CI.

## Result
| | TikTok | YouTube | X |
|---|---|---|---|
| offset target vs final model | +0.001 (-0.002-0.004) | +0.007 (0.003-0.010) | +0.000 (-0.003-0.004) |
| bias lowest fifth, final → offset | +0.275 → +0.246 | +0.491 → +0.457 | +0.629 → +0.622 |
| bias highest fifth, final → offset | -0.223 → -0.210 | -0.456 → -0.441 | -0.585 → -0.569 |
| offset + all context | -0.002 | +0.002 | -0.004 |

## Decision
Refuted: the offset target narrows the bias of the extreme fifths by only 0.01-0.03 (hypothesis was ≥ 0.05) and gains overall only on YouTube (+0.007, small). The compression of extremes is not mainly a tree extrapolation problem; it is regression to the mean from noisy inputs (E18), which a change of target cannot remove. The TikTok gain of TabPFN in > 1,000-reaction posts (E47) is not reproduced.

## Follow-ups
B03 (quantile models) remains the way to address the extremes.
