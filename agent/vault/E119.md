# E119 · Author target encoding as a feature

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: the author's past views are the largest known signal (E68), but they were used as a post-hoc correction.
  Does the author's shrunk mean log views work better as a feature inside CatBoost?
- Hypothesis: a gain on posts whose author appears in training, no harm elsewhere.
- Refuted if: no gain or leakage.

## Setup
- Final CatBoost features + `author_te`: shrunk mean log views of the author, (sum + 5*prior)/(n + 5).
- Encoding computed out-of-fold inside each training fold (inner 5-fold brand CV) and from all training rows for the
  test rows; no test views used.
- 5-fold brand CV; MAE overall and on the subset whose author is seen in training.
- Run: `python src/author_te.py`.

## Result
| platform | seen share | base MAE | +author_te | base, author seen | +author_te, seen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 1.0% | 0.273 | 0.273 | 0.289 | 0.287 |
| YouTube | 8.1% | 0.456 | 0.454 | 0.465 | **0.446** |
| X | 2.8% | 0.467 | 0.464 | 0.530 | **0.430** |

- Overall the gain is ≤ 0.003 because few authors repeat in the sample.
- On posts whose author is seen: X -0.100, YouTube -0.019, TikTok -0.002.

## Decision
Add `author_te` when the author has view history; it is the same signal as E68 and it works as a feature. In this sample
it is limited by author repetition (1-8%), not by the method; for Brand24, which monitors the same authors repeatedly,
this is the largest available lever. Do not expect gains for cold authors.

## Follow-ups
- Combine with a brand-level encoding (E72 says brand ≤ 0.08 of variance).
- The author history could be the *same author on another platform* (E68 used within-platform only).
