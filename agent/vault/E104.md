# E104 · Viral alert at the top 1%

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: reach is dominated by a handful of posts (E96). Can the model flag the top 1% of views from reactions and
  comments, and does it beat a simple ranking by likes or by Brand24's reach?
- Hypothesis: the MAE model ranks the extreme tail at least as well as raw engagement.
- Refuted if: raw likes beat the model at the top 1%.

## Setup
- Per platform, label = views in the top 1% (and 5%); CatBoost classifier on log1p(likes) + log1p(comments),
  5-fold brand-grouped CV.
- Metric: precision in the k highest scores with k = number of positives (= recall there), lift over the base rate, AUC.
- Baselines: raw likes; Brand24 reach (on TikTok/YouTube this is the view count itself, so it is not a fair contest).
- Run: `python src/tail_alert.py`.

## Result
Top 1% (base rate 1%):

| platform | model AUC | model precision | raw likes | Brand24 reach |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 0.990 | 0.56 | **0.64** | 1.00 (is the view count) |
| YouTube | 0.837 | 0.48 | **0.64** | 1.00 (is the view count) |
| X | 0.854 | 0.26 | **0.47** | 0.42 |

Top 5%: model 0.75 / 0.55 / 0.50, likes 0.76 / 0.61 / 0.54, Brand24 1.00 / 1.00 / 0.53.

## Decision
The MAE model is **not** a good extreme-tail ranker: raw likes beat it on every platform, by up to 0.22 precision on
YouTube (0.64 vs 0.48) and 0.21 on X (0.47 vs 0.26). This fits E18 (the model compresses the extremes). For viral
alerts at the 1% level, use engagement or a dedicated precision@k model, not the reach regression. Brand24 is exact on
TikTok/YouTube and 0.42 on X.

## Follow-ups
- A classifier trained with a precision@k objective (or a ranking model) instead of log-loss.
- Alert at the top 0.1%, where the concentration is even stronger (E96).
