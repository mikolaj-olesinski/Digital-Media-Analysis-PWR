# E234 · Are view-model outliers off-brand mentions? And can the off-brand detector be improved?

## Question and hypothesis
- Question 1: are the posts far above or below the final view model (top / bottom 5% of the out-of-fold residual) often off-brand mentions according to the views-free detector of E224?
- Hypothesis 1 (written from the task before the run): outliers score higher on the off-brand detector than the rest (AUC of the score for "outlier" >= 0.55), and removing the flagged posts removes a visible part of the over-performers.
- **Refuted if: AUC of the combined score for "outlier" < 0.55 overall and on every platform** (CI includes 0.5), or the over-performer rate among kept posts stays at 5%.
- Question 2: can an honest, brand-grouped CV on the 287 AI-labelled posts lift the off-brand AUC above the 0.75 bar that E224 missed (0.677)?
- Hypothesis 2: at least one of the methods a-g exceeds 0.75.
- **Refuted if: no method has a grouped-CV AUC above 0.75.** Stricter reading reported too: lower CI bound above 0.75.

## Setup
- Task 1: `results_preds.csv` residual y - pred per platform (TikTok 2,491 / YouTube 2,441 / X 1,803 posts), top and bottom 5% per platform = 339 over + 339 under; rows matched to `mention_id` per platform as in `size_slices.py`. Detector = `combined` of `offbrand_scores.csv` (no views). Compared: mean score percentile, share in the top 10% of the score, AUC of the score for "outlier" (post bootstrap CI), labelled posts among outliers. Views lost: sum of views of flagged posts (top 5% / top 10% of the score) over all views of the three platforms.
- Task 2: 287 labelled posts (221 not_about_brand), 128 brands; repeated brand-grouped 5-fold CV (5 random group splits, OOF scores averaged), AUC CI by post bootstrap, design-weighted AUC as in E224. Logistic regression on standardised features. Methods: (a) stack of five detector percentiles; (b) name ambiguity (share of other brands' texts that contain the name, name length, words, hand flag from `brand_industry.csv`); (c) Louvain resolution 0.5/1/2 x k 5/10/20/40 x two graphs (24 configs), the best config chosen on the training folds only; (d) author level (distinct brands per author, posts, mean score of the author's other posts); (e) text features (hashtag count and share, length, name only inside a hashtag, ASCII share and English stop-word share as a language proxy); (f) label propagation on the e5 10-NN graph from training labels only, alone and added to the stack; (g) Platt and isotonic calibration of the (b) model with a reliability table; h) all of a + b + d + e. Precision in top 5% / 10% is taken inside the 287 labelled posts on the out-of-fold score (14 / 29 posts, enriched sample), not the population top 5% of E224, so it is not comparable with the 0.78 of E224.
- `src/offbrand_more.py` (about one minute, local). Example texts are printed to the terminal for reading by hand and are not stored.

## Results
Numbers: `offbrand_more_numbers.json`; labels are AI labels.

**Task 1 - outliers are not more off-brand.** Outliers (n = 678) have mean score percentile 0.493 vs 0.520 for the rest; share in the top 10% of the score 0.100 vs 0.110; AUC of the score for "outlier" 0.474 (0.451-0.497). Over-performers (n = 339) are even less suspicious: percentile 0.471, top-10% share 0.068, AUC 0.452 (0.422-0.482). Under-performers: 0.515, 0.133, AUC 0.498 (0.470-0.532). By platform (TikTok / YouTube / X) AUC over 0.454 / 0.435 / 0.468, under 0.517 / 0.453 / 0.533, all outliers 0.485 / 0.441 / 0.501. Only X under-performers lean off-brand (top-10% share 0.198, AUC 0.533, CI 0.472-0.596, includes 0.5).
- Labelled posts among outliers: 11 (of 164 labelled on the three platforms): 7 not_about_brand, 3 about_brand, 1 unclear = 64% vs 80% among the other labelled posts. Over 3 of 5, under 4 of 6. The numbers are tiny; no enrichment.
- Views lost if the flagged posts are dropped: top 5% of the score (346 posts, 5.1%) = **2.3% of the views** (TikTok 1.1% / YouTube 3.5% / X 7.9%); top 10% (732 posts) = 6.5% (3.9% / 9.5% / 20.9%). Flagged posts are 3.2% of the over-performers (6.8% with the top 10% flag) and 5.9% of the under-performers; the over-performer rate among the kept posts stays 0.051 (was 0.050) and 0.053 with the 10% flag, so the outliers do not lose importance. The 95th residual percentile moves by at most 0.02 log10.

**Two paraphrased cases (no quotes, no handles, no URL).**
1. Over-performer, X, brand AAA: a post about a survival-creature game says it feels low-budget and that the publisher of the studio's big-budget titles is mostly irrelevant; "AAA" is the video-game tier, not the auto club. About 1,200 views against an estimate of about 4 (x296). Mechanism: an acronym that is an ordinary word of another domain; a gaming thread reached far more people than a reaction-free post suggests.
2. Under-performer, X, brand Tripadvisor: a generic, review-summary style post about a run-down amusement venue lists several review sites (the matched brand is one item in a list of review platforms). 9 views against an estimate of about 520 (x0.017). Mechanism: a list of brands; the post is not about the brand, and with no reactions its reach is far below what the author's profile suggests.
Another over-performer (YouTube, Dodge, 6,157 vs 121, x51) is a workout video where "dodge" is a verb.

- (c) The in-fold chosen config reaches 0.731; the best single config on all labels (e5, k = 20, resolution 2) has 0.740, which is optimistic and was not used. Higher resolution and k = 20-40 help the e5 graph; TF-IDF graphs do not (0.63-0.69).
- (d) The author-level signal points the wrong way across folds (AUC 0.34, i.e. the sign flips between brand groups): authors who mention many brands are not more off-brand in this sample.
- (g) Calibration: prevalence 0.77 in the enriched sample; Brier 0.173 raw, 0.173 Platt, 0.171 isotonic vs 0.177 for the constant; ECE 0.076 raw, 0.053 Platt, 0.107 isotonic. The model barely beats the constant in Brier; the reliability table is in the JSON. The calibrated scores' AUC (0.65) is below the raw one only because the per-fold maps differ; it is not a better detector. The probabilities are valid for this sample (77% off-brand), not for the population.
- Precision in the top 5% / 10% is 0.93-1.00 for every trained stack but rests on 14 / 29 posts and an enriched sample (the untrained combined score gets 0.57 / 0.69 on the same definition, 0.78 / 0.84 on the E224 population definition).

## Decision
- Task 1: **hypothesis 1 is refuted** - off-brand mentions are not what makes the view model miss. Over-performers are, if anything, less suspicious than the rest; flagging the top 5% costs 2.3% of the views and leaves the outlier mix unchanged. Outliers need other explanations (E184 promo and spam, E232 not predictable). The two cases show the mechanisms (domain word, brand list) but they are anecdotes.
- Task 2: **no method clears 0.75 in a way that can be relied on.** One row (e stack + text features) has a point estimate of 0.766 and a design-weighted 0.762, but its lower CI bound is 0.70, 14 variants were tried on the same 287 labels, the labels are AI labels, and the text features alone give 0.684. Treat it as a candidate for confirmation on a new labelled sample (B31), not as a result; the graph grid (0.731) and all-features model (0.747) are also close but below. Author aggregation, ambiguity, label propagation and calibration do not help. The E224 detector stays the flagging tool of record.
