# E174 · Platform time: the platform's recent level and its Wikipedia attention

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: does a platform-wide good or bad week (mean views per reaction of posts checked in the previous 7 / 30 days) or attention to the platform itself (its English Wikipedia article) explain views?
- Hypothesis: platform-level swings (algorithm changes, app popularity) move all posts together.
- Refuted if: paired gains with CIs at or below zero in brand CV and on later months.

## Setup
- Columns: platform mean views per reaction over posts checked in the previous 7 / 30 days (at least 5 posts), and the platform article's daily pageviews (post day and 7-day mean vs the 60-day median; only the five platform names were sent to Wikimedia). Coverage 43-56% for all columns together.
- Every time column uses only other posts whose view check was before the post (no own views, no future).
- Evaluation: final CatBoost (`em.ENG`) vs + columns, same brand folds, paired brand bootstrap; and a temporal split
  (train before 2026-06-01, test after, 1,253-1,588 test posts per platform). Run: `python src/time_momentum.py` via Kueue.

## Result
- Brand CV gain (TikTok / YouTube / X, 95% CI): -0.001 (-0.004 to +0.001) / -0.010 (-0.015 to -0.005) / -0.007 (-0.013 to -0.001).
- Temporal (train before 2026-06-01, test after): -0.005 (-0.011 to +0.001) / -0.059 (-0.070 to -0.047) / -0.013 (-0.023 to -0.004).
- All four groups together: brand CV -0.001 (-0.004 to +0.003) / -0.019 (-0.027 to -0.011) / +0.001 (-0.006 to +0.008); temporal -0.011 (-0.018 to -0.005) / -0.099 (-0.114 to -0.078) / -0.011 (-0.022 to +0.001).
- YouTube's temporal test loses 0.03-0.10 with any added columns (age-at-check shift, E62), so temporal YouTube gains are not informative for small effects.

## Decision
Refuted. Platform-wide time swings carry no signal beyond the post's own reactions; consistent with E46/E133/E136 (no drift).
