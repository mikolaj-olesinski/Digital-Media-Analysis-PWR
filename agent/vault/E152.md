# E152 · Error by brand size and industry

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: are some brands or industries systematically harder? Does brand size predict the error?
- Hypothesis: bigger brands are harder (more variance); media/entertainment is the hardest industry.
- Refuted if: no relation and no industry spread.

## Setup
- Out-of-fold residuals of the final model (5-fold brand CV), aggregated per brand (>= 10 posts) and per industry
  (>= 20 posts); industry from `brand_industry.csv`.
- Run: `python src/error_segments.py`.

## Result
- Brand size vs error: TikTok +0.134 (p = 0.13), YouTube -0.170 (p = 0.07), X -0.048 (p = 0.72): weak, not
  significant.
- Industry spread: TikTok 0.197-0.421 (0.224), YouTube 0.388-0.686 (0.298), X 0.404-0.648 (0.244).
- Hardest: TikTok sportswear 0.421, YouTube beverages 0.686, X social media 0.648; media and entertainment is in the
  worst three on all platforms.
- Easiest: TikTok consumer goods 0.197, YouTube telecom/retail 0.388, X food 0.404.

## Decision
Industry matters at the margin (0.22-0.30 MAE between the easiest and hardest), and the hardest industries are the
viral-prone ones (media, social media). Brand size is not a useful difficulty signal. Use this for segment-level
caveats, not for a new model feature (E84: industry adds <= 0.005).
