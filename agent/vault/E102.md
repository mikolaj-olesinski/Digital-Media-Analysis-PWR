# E102 · Brand scorecard: ranking vs level

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: if Brand24 ranked brands and flagged the top 10% of posts by its reach, how often would it be right, and
  does a views model do better?
- Hypothesis: the model wins because Brand24's rule is ×9 off on X.
- Refuted if: the rule ranks as well as the model where it has no views.

## Setup
- Platforms with views (TikTok 2 491, YouTube 2 441, X 1 803 posts). Brand24 reach vs ridge on likes + comments
  (out-of-fold, brand-grouped) vs measured views.
- Brand totals for brands with >= 5 posts: Kendall tau against the true total, top-5 overlap.
- Alerts: precision of the top 10% by score against the true top 10% of views; AUC.
- Run: `python src/brand_scorecard.py`.

## Result
| platform | brands | Kendall b24 | Kendall model | top-5 b24 / model | alert precision b24 / model / likes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 238 | 1.00 | 0.78 | 5/5, 3/5 | 1.00 / 0.81 / 0.80 |
| YouTube | 213 | 1.00 | 0.65 | 5/5, 2/5 | 1.00 / 0.68 / 0.71 |
| X | 189 | 0.63 | 0.58 | 4/5, 4/5 | 0.61 / 0.58 / 0.58 |

On TikTok and YouTube `b24_estimated_reach` **is** the view count, so perfect scores there are by construction, not a
fair contest. On X, where the rule is a real estimate, its brand ranking (Kendall 0.63) and alert precision (0.61) are
slightly **better** than the ridge model (0.58 / 0.58), although its level is ×9.3 too high (E99).

## Decision
Brand24's reach is a good ranker where it copies views and a decent ranker on X; the model does not beat it on ranking
and can be worse on brand-total order (YouTube 2/5 top-5). The model's value is the **calibrated level and intervals**,
not the ordering. Frame the product that way: fix the X level (E99), keep the ranking, add intervals (E98).

## Follow-ups
- A dedicated ranking model (pairwise/listwise) could beat both; not tried.
- Alert precision at 1% is in E104.
