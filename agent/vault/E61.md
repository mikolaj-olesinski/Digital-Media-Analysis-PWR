# E61 · One pooled model for the three platforms, with monotone constraints

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: does one CatBoost for TikTok / YouTube / X (platform as a category, 3× the data) beat the per-platform final models? Do monotone constraints (more followers or reactions never lower the estimate) help in-platform or in leave-one-platform-out transfer, where ridge on likes + comments has been best (E13)?
- Hypothesis: pooled ≈ per-platform (±0.01); monotone constraints cost ≤ 0.005 in-platform and make LOPO CatBoost at least as good as LOPO ridge.

## Setup
- Joint 5 brand-grouped folds over the three platforms. Per platform (final model), per platform monotone, pooled with platform category, pooled monotone.
- LOPO: train on the other two platforms without the test fold's brands, no platform column: ridge on likes + comments (E13), CatBoost, CatBoost monotone.
- Paired brand bootstrap CI.

## Result
| | TikTok | YouTube | X |
|---|---|---|---|
| per platform (final model) | 0.271 | 0.453 | 0.467 |
| per platform, monotone | 0.274 (-0.004) | 0.479 (-0.026) | 0.478 (-0.011) |
| pooled with platform column | 0.272 (-0.001) | 0.465 (-0.012) | 0.471 (-0.004) |
| pooled, monotone | 0.277 (-0.007) | 0.496 (-0.043) | 0.484 (-0.017) |
| LOPO ridge on likes + comments (E13) | 0.313 | 0.611 | 0.650 |
| LOPO CatBoost | 0.412 | 0.632 | 0.706 |
| LOPO CatBoost, monotone | 0.425 | 0.615 | 0.722 |

All pooled and monotone gains have CIs below zero except pooled on TikTok and X (CI to 0.000-0.002); LOPO CatBoost is worse than ridge by 0.02-0.10.

## Decision
Refuted: pooling the three platforms does not help (the platforms differ more than more data helps), monotone constraints cost up to 0.026 in-platform (on YouTube more followers does not always mean more views), and neither makes CatBoost transfer as well as the simple ridge. Ridge on likes + comments stays the transfer model.
