# E87 · Mapping reaction scales between platforms before the out-of-platform model

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: platforms differ in how many likes and comments a post gets. Does mapping the target platform's reactions onto the source platforms' scale (using only the target's inputs, no views) improve out-of-platform estimates? This is a form of calibration that would work on FB / IG.
- Hypothesis: distribution matching (quantile) or within-platform standardisation lowers the out-of-platform MAE.
- Refuted if: raw inputs stay best.

## Setup
- Leave one platform out on TikTok / YouTube / X: train on the other two, test on the third, target-fold brands removed from training (as E13). Inputs: log likes, comments, shares, followers. Mappings: none; z-score within each platform; quantile (target value -> pooled-source value at the same percentile); scale-free ratios (comments per like, shares per like, likes per follower, within-platform percentiles). Ridge on likes + comments and CatBoost on all four. Then a shift fitted on 10 / 25 / 50 target posts with views (E15).

## Result
- Without a shift, raw inputs are best: ridge 0.312 / 0.604 / 0.641. Quantile and z-score mapping are much worse: 0.76-1.04 TikTok, 0.61-0.65 YouTube, 1.28-1.43 X (bias up to ±1.4). Ratios: 0.37 / 0.73 / 0.73.
- Ranking barely changes: Spearman 0.74-0.86 TikTok, 0.62-0.73 YouTube, 0.38-0.59 X; CatBoost z-score ranks YouTube best (0.73 vs 0.65).
- After a shift on 25-50 target posts: X CatBoost ratios 0.521-0.528 and z-score 0.524-0.532 vs ridge raw 0.552-0.560 (about +0.03); TikTok and YouTube raw ridge stays best (0.316-0.320, 0.608-0.619).

## Decision
Refuted. Mapping reaction scales between platforms without views makes the estimates much worse: the absolute number of likes carries the view level across platforms better than a post's rank within its platform. Only with 25-50 calibration posts does a mapped CatBoost gain about +0.03 on X. For FB / IG keep raw likes and comments.
