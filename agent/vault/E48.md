# E48 · Classification: which posts go viral, and which have no view count?

## Question and hypothesis
- Question 1 (B08): is a post in the top 10% of views of its platform? A ranking question Brand24 can use for alerts.
- Hypothesis 1: the final regression model ranks viral posts as well as a dedicated classifier (ROC AUC within 0.02), and both beat followers alone and the Brand24 reach column by ≥ 0.05 AUC.
- Question 2: 9 / 32 / 63 posts have a view count of 0, and 634 X / 27 YouTube posts have none. They are dropped. Is the missing count predictable from what is known (post date, age at the check, reactions, followers)?
- Hypothesis 2: missing views on X are mostly old posts or posts with missing reactions (a collection artefact), so a classifier on held-out brands reaches ROC AUC ≥ 0.8.
- Question 3: what does the final model predict for the zero-view posts? Hypothesis: it predicts tens to hundreds of views, i.e. zero counts are not reachable from reactions.

## Setup
- Data: TikTok / YouTube / X. Viral: posts with views > 0 (2,491 / 2,441 / 1,803); threshold = 90th percentile of the training fold.
- Scores: log followers, log Brand24 reach, out-of-fold final regression model, CatBoost classifier (same ENG features). Metrics: ROC AUC, average precision, precision among the 10% top-scored, brand bootstrap CIs. 5-fold brand-grouped CV.
- No views: CatBoost classifier present vs zero/missing on post date, age at the check, followers, reactions, missing flags, text length; brand-grouped CV; TikTok skipped (9 posts).

## Result
**Viral (top 10% of views of the platform, threshold from the training fold), held-out brands:**

| score | TikTok AUC | TikTok P@10% | YouTube AUC | YouTube P@10% | X AUC | X P@10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| followers | 0.753 | 36% | 0.836 | 44% | 0.774 | 43% |
| Brand24 reach | (= views) | | (= views) | | 0.899 | 62% |
| regression (final model) | 0.983 | 83% (72-89) | 0.935 | 71% (61-80) | 0.894 | 63% (54-71) |
| classifier (CatBoost) | 0.977 | 80% | 0.925 | 71% | 0.896 | 63% |

Average precision of the regression: 0.90 / 0.79 / 0.68. Chance is 10%. On TikTok and YouTube the Brand24 reach column equals the view count (100% of rows), so it is not a score there.

**No view count (TikTok / YouTube / X posts):** zero 9 / 32 / 63, missing 0 / 27 / 634.
- On X, posts from 2025 almost never have views: 113 missing + 45 zero against 12 present. In 2026: 521 missing + 18 zero against 1,791 present.
- Missing X views are checked later (median age at the check 66 days vs 21 days); zero X views are old posts (median posted 2025-02, 139 days).
- A classifier on held-out brands tells missing/zero from present with ROC AUC 0.949 (0.933-0.969) on YouTube (likes, age, followers) and 0.910 (0.887-0.930) on X (age at the check, likes, post date).
- The final model, trained on views > 0, predicts a median of 87 / 9 / 15 views for the zero-view posts; all zero-view TikTok and YouTube posts have no likes or comments.

## Decision
Hypothesis 1 supported: the regression model ranks viral posts as well as a dedicated classifier (AUC within 0.01) and far better than followers (+0.12 to +0.23 AUC); on X it matches the Brand24 rule, which itself uses the reaction count. No separate viral classifier is needed: rank by the regression estimate. Hypothesis 2 supported: missing and zero views are predictable (AUC 0.91-0.95) and on X depend on the post date and the age at the check, which points to a collection artefact (old X posts lose or never get a view count), not to posts nobody saw. Dropping them is defensible; they should not be read as true zeros. Hypothesis 3: zero-view posts get small predictions (9-15 views on YouTube and X), close to zero on the log scale, so the model is not badly wrong about them.
