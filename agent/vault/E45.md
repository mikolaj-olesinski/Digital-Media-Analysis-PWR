# E45 · Do the newer checks in the history file make views more predictable?

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: 1,230 posts have a later check (`current_*`); in about a third of them views grew. Does replacing a post's views, reactions and age with its latest check change how well the final model does?
- Hypothesis: older posts are closer to their final views, so the refreshed data are slightly more predictable (lower MAE on the refreshed posts).
- Refuted if: MAE on the refreshed posts does not fall (CI includes zero).

## Setup
- Data: same rows as E44. Refreshed copy: for posts with `current_views`, views, likes, comments, shares and age are taken from the latest check (missing current reactions keep the old value).
- Base: final model + age (E44 new) trained and tested on the original data.
- New: the same model trained and tested on the refreshed data.
- Compared on the same posts and folds; reported on all posts and on the refreshed subset. Because the target changes with the data, this measures predictability, not a model gain.

## Result
| On the refreshed posts | TikTok | YouTube | X |
|---|---|---|---|
| posts | 348 | 503 | 342 |
| views grew at the newer check | 133 | 201 | 33 |
| MAE original → refreshed | 0.287 → 0.296 | 0.435 → 0.429 | 0.417 → 0.463 |
| change (CI) | -0.010 (-0.017 to -0.002) | +0.006 (-0.011-0.020) | -0.046 (-0.066 to -0.027) |

On X, likes changed in 1% of refreshed posts and views in 10%: the target moves while the inputs stay.

## Decision
Hypothesis refuted. The newer checks do not make the main model better; on X they make it worse, because reactions are not re-measured as often as views. Keep the original columns as the training data.
