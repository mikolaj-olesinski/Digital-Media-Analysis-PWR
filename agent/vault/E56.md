# E56 · Noise ceiling: how different are the views of posts with almost the same inputs?

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: is MAE 0.27 / 0.45 / 0.47 close to what any model can reach with followers and reactions?
- Method: pairs of posts from different brands on the same platform whose log followers, likes, comments and shares all differ by ≤ 0.05. For two independent draws from the same conditional distribution, E|y1 − y2| lies between the best possible MAE and twice it, so the best MAE on those posts is at least half the mean difference.
- Hypothesis: the final model is within 0.15 of that lower bound on every platform (little left to gain from these inputs).

## Setup
- Nearest neighbours (Chebyshev distance on the four log inputs) within the platform, first neighbour from another brand within 0.05.
- Final model out-of-fold MAE on the paired posts and on all posts.
- Caveat: matched posts are not a random sample (easy to match: few reactions).

## Result
| | TikTok | YouTube | X |
|---|---|---|---|
| matched pairs (posts) | 183 (304) | 639 (866) | 999 (1,301) |
| mean \|Δ log views\| in a pair | 0.446 | 0.775 | 0.666 |
| lower bound on the best MAE (½ mean \|Δ\|) | 0.223 | 0.388 | 0.333 |
| final model MAE on these posts | 0.325 | 0.578 | 0.471 |
| final model MAE, all posts | 0.274 | 0.454 | 0.467 |

## Decision
Hypothesis supported on TikTok (gap 0.10) and X (0.14), not on YouTube (0.19). The bound is loose (the best MAE lies between ½ and 1 × the mean difference), so these gaps are upper limits on what better models could gain from followers and reactions alone: posts with practically the same inputs differ by ×2.8 / ×6.0 / ×4.6 in views. Most of the remaining error is in the data, not the model. Matched posts are mostly low-reaction posts, which are harder than average.
