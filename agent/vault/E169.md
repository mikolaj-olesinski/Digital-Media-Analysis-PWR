# E169 · If the author's history is unknown, can the brand's history substitute?

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: when a post's author has no earlier posts, can the brand's own history (its mean views in the training
  period) replace the author prior?
- Hypothesis: a brand prior recovers part of the gain, because big brands have bigger audiences than their follower
  counts suggest.
- Refuted if: adding the brand prior does not lower MAE on later posts.

## Setup
- Temporal split: train on posts before 2026-06-01, test on later posts (the realistic client setting: brand history
  exists, future posts do not).
- Base: CatBoost MAE on followers, reactions, missing flags, log(age). Plus: the same features + a smoothed brand prior
  (brand mean log10 views in train, k=5, global mean for unseen brands).
- Paired gain with a 200-sample brand bootstrap.
- Run: `python src/brand_prior.py`.

## Result
| platform | base MAE | + brand prior | gain (95% CI) | share of test posts with a known brand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 0.276 | 0.281 | **-0.005** (-0.011 to -0.002) | 51.7% |
| YouTube | 0.481 | 0.517 | **-0.035** (-0.054 to -0.017) | 47.5% |
| X | 0.520 | 0.514 | +0.006 (-0.010 to +0.024) | 34.9% |

- The brand prior **hurts** on TikTok and YouTube and is neutral on X.
- Only 35-52% of test posts have a brand seen in training, and the temporal split itself raises MAE (0.276 / 0.481 /
  0.520 against 0.273 / 0.456 / 0.454 in brand CV).

## Decision
Brand history is **not** a substitute for author history. The author's own past views are a per-account level effect
(E119: X 0.530 -> 0.430 on seen authors, YouTube 0.465 -> 0.446), which a brand average drowns out. Without author
history the model stays the base model; the realistic substitutes are (a) one earlier **view** check of the same post
(growth curve 0.165 / 0.188 / 0.269 MAE, E40), and (b) 25-50 client posts for calibration on X (E115).
