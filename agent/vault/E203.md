# E203 · Ideas from the cross-domain literature: post age and brand prominence on other platforms

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: Do two signals the literature reports as transferable improve the X + YouTube -> FB / IG transfer: (a) post age at read (temporal signal, Shulman et al. 2016; SMTPD 2025), (b) the brand's residual on other platforms (cross-domain auxiliary data, Tatar et al. 2014; Roy et al. 2013)?
- Hypothesis: (a) helps if reactions per day carry the view level; (b) helps if a brand over- or under-performs consistently across platforms.

## Setup
- Base: ridge on log likes + log comments trained on X + YouTube (E93 / E202). FB 402 videos, IG 338 reels.
- (a) + log age at read. (b) brand median residual of the same ridge on TikTok / YouTube / X / all three, added with weight 0.5 or 1. `python src/cross_domain_aux.py` (seconds).

## Result
| MAE (typical error) | FB | IG |
|---|---|---|
| base | 0.385 (x2.43) | 0.424 (x2.65) |
| (a) + log age | 0.406 (x2.55) | 0.381 (x2.41) |
| (b) brand residual, TikTok, w 0.5 | 0.398 | 0.446 |
| (b) brand residual, YouTube, w 0.5 | 0.407 | 0.440 |
| (b) brand residual, X, w 0.5 | 0.502 | 0.508 |
| (b) brand residual, all three, w 0.5 | 0.416 | 0.446 |

- Weight 1 is worse than weight 0.5 everywhere. Brand coverage 60-100%.
- Age: FB / IG posts were read at a median of 310 / 343 days, the training views at 23 days, so the age term extrapolates. It removes the IG level bias (+0.25 -> -0.02) and adds one on FB (+0.08 -> -0.17): it acts as a level shift, not as a velocity signal.

## Decision
Refuted. Brand prominence from other platforms hurts FB and IG; age is inconsistent (helps IG, hurts FB) and out of the training range. Reactions read with the views stay the only transferable signal. A true velocity test needs reactions read early in a post's life, which we do not have for FB / IG.

## Follow-ups
- Velocity needs two reads of a fresh post (E40-style) on FB / IG.
