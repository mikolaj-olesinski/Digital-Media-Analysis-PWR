# E108 · Confidence flags from metadata

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: can the fields known before the check (missing likes/comments/shares, zero reactions, age of the check,
  followers, platform, Brand24 reach) tell which posts will have a large error? If yes, a product can flag or re-check
  the risky mentions.
- Hypothesis: error is mostly noise (E56), so the flags will be weak.
- Refuted if: the AUC is close to 0.5 and flagging removes little error.

## Setup
- 6 735 posts with views (TikTok, YouTube, X); error of the views model (ridge on likes + comments, out-of-fold by
  brand); "big error" = |error| > 0.5 (worse than ×3).
- CatBoost classifier on metadata only; 5-fold brand CV; AUC.
- Flag the top 20 / 30 / 50% by risk: share of the total absolute error they carry, MAE on the unflagged posts.
- Run: `python src/confidence_flags.py`.

## Result
- Big-error share 35.6%; **AUC 0.936**.
- Flagging 20%: 45% of the total error, MAE 0.484 -> 0.333.
- Flagging 30%: 57.5% of the error, MAE 0.294.
- Flagging 50%: 77.7% of the error, MAE 0.216.

## Decision
The error is very predictable from metadata, contrary to the hypothesis. A confidence flag is a cheap product feature:
flag or re-check the risky mentions, widen their intervals, and keep the rest. The high AUC partly reflects platform
and age (older checks and X are harder), so the flag should be shown with the reason.
