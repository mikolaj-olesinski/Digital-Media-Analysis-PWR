# E101 · Age-normalised views

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: do the platforms and the FB sample differ in the age at which views are counted, and does normalising views
  to a fixed age (30 days) remove the level bias without labels?
- Hypothesis: older posts have more views per reaction; normalising the target makes platforms comparable and trims the
  FB bias (+0.14 on 402 posts, E95).
- Refuted if: the error or the bias stays the same.

## Setup
- Age at check = `metrics_updated_at - created_at`, and for FB the age at the fetch.
- Growth exponent b from log10(views/likes) ~ b log10(age), per platform; the held-out platform uses the mean b of the
  training platforms (no target labels).
- Train ridge on the normalised target, convert the prediction back with the target's age; compare with the standard
  target on the same rows.
- Run: `python src/age_target.py`.

## Result
b: TikTok 0.043, YouTube 0.164, X 0.037.

| target | standard MAE | age-normalised | bias standard | bias normalised |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 0.313 | 0.315 | +0.03 | -0.02 |
| YouTube | 0.612 | 0.603 | +0.21 | +0.21 |
| X | 0.650 | 0.645 | -0.40 | -0.39 |
| **Facebook (402)** | **0.385** | **0.380** | **+0.14** | **0.00** |

## Decision
Age normalisation is not an accuracy gain (<=0.01) but it **removes the FB level bias** (+0.14 -> 0.00): the FB posts
are older than the source posts, so the model looks low on FB because views have grown since. Reporting "views at 30
days" or correcting by age is the honest way to compare platforms. Update findings.
