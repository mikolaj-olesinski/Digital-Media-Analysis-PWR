# E105 · Selection in X view counts

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: 25% of X posts have no view count. Are they a random subset? Can the missingness be predicted? How far does
  training or averaging on the observed posts shift an average-reach estimate for the whole X sample?
- Hypothesis: missingness is not random (old posts, late checks) and the observed subset overstates reach.
- Refuted if: the missing posts look like the observed ones and the averages agree.

## Setup
- X posts: 2 500, observed views 1 866, missing 634 (25.4%).
- Features: log reactions, log followers, age of the check; CatBoost selection model, 5-fold brand-grouped CV.
- Views model (ridge on likes + comments) fitted on the observed posts, applied to every X post.
- Inverse-probability weights 1 / p(observed) to re-weight the observed mean to the full sample.
- Run: `python src/x_selection.py`.

## Result
| | observed | missing |
|---|---|---|
| median followers | 435 | 239 |
| median age at check (days) | 22.2 | 65.9 |
| median reactions | 0 | 0 |

- Share missing by year: 2025 **66.5%**, 2026 **22.4%** — a collection artefact, not random.
- Selection model AUC **0.837**.
- Mean model reach: observed unweighted 435, observed IPW 367, full sample 332. The observed-only average overstates
  the full-sample average by ~1.3x.

## Decision
X view counts are missing not at random: old posts and smaller accounts are under-represented. Any average reach
computed on posts with views overstates the platform by about 30%; report medians, intervals and the share missing, or
weight the observed posts. Update findings.

## Follow-ups
- Is the same pattern on Instagram (no views at all) and Facebook (video only)?
- Use the selection weights in the training of the X model and check the effect on brand totals.
