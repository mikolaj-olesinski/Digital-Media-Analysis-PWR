# E85 · Where exactly promo over-performs

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: promo posts over-perform on average (E04, E83), yet the type label adds only +0.005. For which promo posts is the effect real?
- Hypothesis: the effect sits in promo posts with real engagement from mid-sized accounts (paid boosting); promo from tiny accounts does not over-perform.
- Refuted if: the promo − organic gap is the same in every subgroup.

## Setup
- Our AI type labels (promo vs organic). Promo − organic mean over-performance (true − out-of-fold final model) within subgroups: follower tier, reaction tier, account kind, US vs not, language, industry community (E69); brand-bootstrap 95% CI, ≥ 20 posts per side. Gain of the type label (E84) per type × reaction tier.

## Result
- TikTok: +0.09 overall; +0.18 (0.07-0.27) at 10k-100k followers, +0.09 at 1k-10k, +0.05 (CI crosses zero) under 1k; +0.14 at 11-1000 reactions; not US +0.10 vs US +0.03; food and drink brands +0.20 (0.10-0.31).
- YouTube: +0.05 overall (CI crosses zero), but +0.20 (0.09-0.31) at 11-100 reactions, +0.17 at 101-1000, +0.13 above 1000, and -0.14 at 0 reactions.
- X: +0.02 overall, but +0.20 at 1k-100k followers and -0.15 (-0.25 to -0.05) under 1k followers.
- Label gain is concentrated in promo posts with reactions: YouTube promo with 11+ reactions +0.014 to +0.022, TikTok promo with 101-1000 reactions +0.017, X promo with 1-10 reactions +0.039; about 0 elsewhere.

## Decision
Supported. Promo posts get ×1.3-1.6 more views than reactions suggest only when they come from mid-sized accounts or already have reactions (likely paid boosting or ad placement); promo from tiny accounts or without reactions gets fewer views. The opposite effects cancel in the average, which is why the label helps little overall.
