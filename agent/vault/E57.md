# E57 · Quantile models (10/50/90%) and tail weighting

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: can quantile models give honest intervals for the extremes, and does up-weighting the outer fifths of the training target reduce the compression of extremes (E18) at an acceptable cost?
- Hypothesis: CatBoost 10-90% quantiles cover about 80% overall but under-cover the lowest and highest fifths; tail weighting ×2 lowers the bias of the extreme fifths by ≥ 0.05 and raises overall MAE by ≤ 0.01.

## Setup
- CatBoost MultiQuantile (0.1, 0.5, 0.9), 500 iterations, ENG features; tail-weighted final model (weight 2 for training posts in the lowest and highest fifth); base final model. 5-fold brand CV.
- Coverage and median width of the 10-90% interval overall and per fifth of true views; MAE and bias per fifth.

## Result
| | TikTok | YouTube | X |
|---|---|---|---|
| coverage of the 10-90% interval (nominal 80%) | 73% | 73% | 74% |
| median width | ×5.4 | ×14.1 | ×28.9 |
| coverage, lowest / highest fifth of true views | 74% / 54% | 70% / 53% | 60% / 44% |
| median (q50) vs final model | -0.001 | **+0.007** (0.003-0.010) | -0.001 |
| tail weighting ×2 vs final model | -0.002 | -0.002 | -0.001 |
| bias of the highest fifth, final → tail weighted | -0.223 → -0.220 | -0.456 → -0.454 | -0.585 → -0.588 |

## Decision
Partly supported: quantile intervals under-cover on held-out brands (73-74% instead of 80%) and most at the top fifth (44-54%), so they need conformal calibration (E02, E34) before use. Tail weighting does nothing (bias of extremes moves ≤ 0.02): the compression of extremes is not fixable by weighting (with E51 and E56: it comes from noise in the inputs). The quantile median is a small gain on YouTube.

## Follow-ups
Conformalised quantile regression per reaction tier if intervals are revisited.
