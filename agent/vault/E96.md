# E96 · Reach is a tail phenomenon

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: how concentrated is visibility? What share of a platform's (and a brand's) total views comes from the
  best 1%, 5%, 10% of posts, and how many posts carry 80% of the reach?
- Hypothesis: heavy tail, but brand totals behave like the platform.
- Refuted if: reach is spread evenly.

## Setup
- All posts with views > 0: TikTok 2 491, YouTube 2 441, X 1 803, Facebook 402 (fetched, E95).
- Metrics per platform: share of total views in the top 1/5/10%, Gini, share of posts needed for 80% of views, Hill
  tail index (alpha) on the top 1%.
- Brands with at least 5 measured posts (319 brand-platform pairs, 3 platforms).

## Result
| platform | top 1% | top 5% | top 10% | Gini | posts for 80% | Hill alpha |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 62% | 88% | 94% | 0.95 | 2.6% | 1.03 |
| YouTube | 72% | 90% | 95% | 0.96 | 2.0% | 0.93 |
| X | 60% | 82% | 89% | 0.92 | 4.2% | 1.00 |
| Facebook | 74% | 88% | 93% | 0.95 | 2.7% | 0.73 |

- Brands: median top-1% share 0.54 (p90 0.89); 56% of brands get more than half of their reach from one post;
  median share of posts needed for 80% of a brand's reach: 15%.
- Hill alpha below 1 for YouTube (0.93) and Facebook (0.73) means the sample mean is dominated by the largest posts;
  it is not a stable summary.

## Decision
Reach is not an average phenomenon: 2-4% of posts carry 80% of views and one post can be half of a brand's reach.
For Brand24 this means (a) brand totals need intervals, not points (E73, E79), (b) a monitoring product must catch the
tail, (c) "average reach per post" is misleading. This is a new descriptive result, **solid** on this sample.

## Follow-ups
- Tail-conditioned model: predict whether a post enters the top 1% (classification) and its size.
- Brand totals: how many posts must be monitored to capture 80% of a brand's reach in a month?
