# E106 · Alert budget

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: with a fixed number of alerts per period, how many of the truly viral posts (top 1% of views) does each
  score catch? Complements E104, which fixed the budget at the number of positives.
- Hypothesis: the views model is the best score because it is the most accurate.
- Refuted if: raw likes or Brand24's reach catch more positives at the same budget.

## Setup
- Per platform, positives = top 1% of views. Scores: model probability (CatBoost on likes + comments, brand CV), raw
  likes, Brand24 reach.
- Recall at budgets 10, 25, 50, 100, 200 alerts; alerts needed for 50% recall.
- Run: `python src/alert_budget.py`.

## Result
Alerts needed to catch half of the top 1% (positives: TikTok 25, YouTube 25, X 19):

| platform | model | raw likes | Brand24 reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 20 | **16** | 13 (reach is the view count) |
| YouTube | 26 | **17** | 13 (reach is the view count) |
| X | **117** | **22** | 25 |

On X the model needs 117 alerts for the same recall that likes reach with 22. At a budget of 50 on X: recall 0.37
(model), 0.63 (likes), 0.74 (Brand24).

## Decision
The MAE model is the worst alert engine at the 1% level; raw likes or the existing reach score are far better, and on
TikTok/YouTube Brand24's reach is the view count itself. For alerts, use engagement or a model trained for precision@k;
keep the calibrated model for levels and intervals (E93, E98). Update findings.

## Follow-ups
- Precision@k objective / learning-to-rank for the alert use case.
- The same curve at the top 0.1% (too few positives in this sample: 2-3 per platform).
