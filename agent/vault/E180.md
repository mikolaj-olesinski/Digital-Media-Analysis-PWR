# E180 · Models for totals instead of single posts

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: Does a loss aimed at the tail (RMSE, tail-weighted MAE, quantile 0.7) make brand and brand-month totals more accurate than the final MAE model?
- Hypothesis: A tail-aware loss lowers the totals' error.

## Setup
- Brand 5-fold CV per platform, ENG features. Totals = sum of 10^prediction per brand and per brand-month (>= 5 posts). Also top 1% of views and Brand24's rule.
- Part of the critical review batch: `python src/critical_review.py` (via Kueue; E178 locally).

## Result
| platform · model | post MAE | top 1% bias | brand total median |log ratio| | brands off > x2 | brand-months off > x2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok MAE (final) | 0.273 | -0.36 | 0.192 | 29% | 27% |
| TikTok RMSE | 0.285 | -0.32 | 0.185 | 27% | 26% |
| TikTok quantile 0.7 | 0.307 | -0.24 | 0.199 | 29% | 30% |
| YouTube MAE (final) | 0.456 | -1.16 | 0.231 | 37% | 39% |
| YouTube RMSE | 0.463 | -1.00 | 0.215 | 37% | 40% |
| YouTube quantile 0.7 | 0.512 | -0.97 | 0.213 | 35% | 36% |
| X MAE (final) | 0.466 | -0.97 | 0.286 | 47% | 48% |
| X RMSE | 0.488 | -0.99 | 0.296 | 49% | 50% |
| X Brand24 rule | 1.060 | +0.22 | 1.055 | 96% | 93% |

- Tail-weighted MAE is worse everywhere. On TikTok / YouTube the rule is the view count (0 error).

## Decision
Refuted. No loss fixes the totals: the best change is 0.016 log10 (YouTube RMSE) and it costs post accuracy; the tail stays x9-14 under-predicted on YouTube / X. Totals need intervals and calibration (E109), not another loss.
