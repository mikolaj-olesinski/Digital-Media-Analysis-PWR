# E47 · Tabular foundation models (TabPFN v2, TabICL) against and with the final model

## Question and hypothesis
- Question: pretrained tabular transformers predict from the training rows in context, with no tuning. On our small, noisy, few-feature tables, do they beat the final CatBoost model or add to it in an average?
- Hypothesis (from a scratch pilot on 2026-11-04 with one seed and 4 estimators: YouTube +0.014, CI 0.007-0.020; TikTok +0.005; X +0.002): with more estimators and three seeds, TabPFN v2 beats CatBoost on YouTube by ≥ 0.01 with a CI above zero, and the average CatBoost + TabPFN + TabICL is at least as good as the best single model on every platform.
- Refuted if: the YouTube gain falls below 0.01 or its CI includes zero, or the gain sits only in posts with zero likes and comments (E27, possibly missing data).
- Note: the pilot looked at the data once, including the choice of median output; this run fixes the settings in advance (median output for TabPFN, mean for TabICL, n_estimators 8, seeds 0/1/2).

## Setup
- Data: TikTok / YouTube / X posts with views > 0; features `extra_models.ENG` (followers + reactions + missing flags); 5-fold brand-grouped CV (`extra_models.cv_predict`), the same folds for every model.
- Base: final model (`reaction_tiers.FINAL`, CatBoost MAE, 300 it.), three seeds averaged.
- New: TabPFN v2 regressor (weights `tabpfn-v2-regressor.ckpt`, Apache-2.0 with attribution "Built with PriorLabs-TabPFN"; not the 2.5+ weights, which are non-commercial), median of the predictive distribution; TabICL v2 regressor (BSD-3). Each averaged over three seeds. CPU only, data never leave homelab3.
- Ensembles: mean(CatBoost, TabPFN), mean(CatBoost, TabICL), mean of all three.
- Breakdown: gain on posts with zero likes and comments vs the rest; gain per reaction tier.
- Paired gains with brand bootstrap CI (`history.gain_ci`).

## Result
| | TikTok | YouTube | X |
|---|---|---|---|
| posts | 2,491 | 2,441 | 1,803 |
| CatBoost (final model, 3 seeds) | 0.273 | 0.453 | 0.467 |
| TabPFN v2 | 0.268 | 0.443 | 0.465 |
| TabICL | 0.272 | 0.445 | 0.466 |
| mean of all three | 0.269 | 0.443 | 0.464 |
| gain TabPFN (CI) | +0.005 (0.003-0.008) | +0.010 (0.004-0.017) | +0.002 (-0.003-0.007) |
| gain mean of three (CI) | +0.004 (0.002-0.006) | +0.010 (0.006-0.014) | +0.003 (-0.000-0.006) |
| gain mean of three, zero likes and comments | +0.005 (90 posts, CI incl. 0) | +0.014 (665 posts, 0.006-0.022) | +0.000 (991 posts) |
| gain mean of three, with reactions | +0.004 | +0.009 (0.004-0.014) | +0.005 (0.000-0.011) |

By reaction tier (mean of three): TikTok > 1,000 reactions +0.026 (0.012-0.040), other tiers ≤ 0.005; YouTube 0 and 1-10 reactions +0.014 / +0.016, higher tiers CI includes zero.
Time on 6 CPU, 3 seeds × 5 folds: TabPFN 136-176 s, TabICL 140-221 s per platform; CatBoost 1 s. The pilot (one seed, 4 estimators) gave YouTube +0.014; with three seeds it is +0.010.

## Decision
Hypothesis partly supported: the YouTube gain is +0.010 with a CI above zero, right at the "small" line, and not only from the zero-reaction posts. The mean of three is not better than TabPFN alone, so the second half of the hypothesis holds only as "about equal, with tighter CIs". It does not change any conclusion; the final model stays CatBoost. If a team wants the last 0.01 on YouTube, use the mean of CatBoost + TabPFN v2 + TabICL and add the TabPFN attribution. The TikTok gain in > 1,000-reaction posts fits the tree extrapolation problem (E18), so B18 (offset target) may get the same for free.

## Follow-ups
B18 offset target (does it reproduce the high-reaction gain with CatBoost alone?); TabPFN with the extra feature groups (text, calendar) is not tested.
