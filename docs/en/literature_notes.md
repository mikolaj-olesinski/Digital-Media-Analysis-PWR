## Transfer methods: what the literature says
1. **Plain ERM is very hard to beat under natural shift.** DomainBed (images), WILDS, Wild-Time, TableShift (tabular classification) and Wild-Tab (tabular regression) all find that tuned ERM matches or beats specialised DG/DA methods. TableShift finds that robustness methods trade ID accuracy for a smaller gap, without better OOD accuracy.
2. **Tabular shifts are mostly Y|X shifts** (WhyShift), and the gap tracks the label shift (TableShift). Covariate-shift tools (importance weighting, CORAL, DANN, input mapping) cannot fix a shifted intercept. That matches what we found.
3. **With few source domains, invariance methods have little to work with.** Linear causal and invariance methods (anchor regression, stabilised regression, invariant subsets) are the most appropriate family for small tabular linear models. Their main practical output is often a justification that a sparse model is near-invariant.
4. **A few target labels are worth more than any unsupervised trick.** Hypothesis-transfer and hierarchical-shrinkage results support estimating a platform offset (shrunken) from 5-50 labels, and a slope only once k is roughly 50 or more. This agrees with the finding that a 25-50 post shift removes most of the bias.
5. **For Facebook and Instagram, where no labels will exist,** the honest deliverable is calibrated *uncertainty*: hierarchical or robust conformal intervals whose width reflects the between-platform offset variance seen across TikTok, YouTube and X.

## Views and reach: what the scan suggests
- **No published views-per-like law to borrow.** I found no peer-reviewed paper that estimates a cross-platform views-on-likes scaling law or views per like. Platform-specific evidence covers:
  - follower elasticities (Munger et al.);
  - the one Facebook true-audience validation, which reports low R2 and concave growth with feedback (Bernstein et al. 2013);
  - X views-per-retweet medians (Galeazzi et al.);
  - one unbiased TikTok sample (Steel et al.).
- **Benchmarks are inconsistent.** Vendor benchmarks (Socialinsider, Rival IQ, OverseerOS) cover brand accounts, mix means and medians, and disagree with each other (for example, two Rival IQ TikTok follower ERs, 1.73% vs 5.69%, appear in secondary sources). Use them only as order-of-magnitude priors: IG about 10, TikTok about 15-30, YouTube about 25-50 and X about 30-200 views per like.
- **The literature agrees with our results.** Reactions beat followers within posts. The relationship is sub-linear or concave. Past author performance is the strongest extra signal, and vendors (Rival IQ) use it too. Large per-post noise remains.
- **Meta's metric is usable for Reels and video.** Since Nov 2024 (FB) and Apr 2025 (IG API), Meta's "views" are plays or on-screen counts that include repeats. Public Reels and FB video play counts are therefore a legitimate, definition-compatible validation target. This is the best available route to test transfer to Meta.
